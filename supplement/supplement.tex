\textbf{Band definitions used in the per-band panels.} Age bands are
Y ($\le 50$), M (51--89), L ($\ge 90$), following the cohort's verified
longevity structure. In the external figures the age-band panels group each
cohort's samples as out-of-support-young (below 50), in-support (50--89), and,
for the microarray cohort, 90+; the support-coverage donuts print the exact
arithmetic (32/64 and 27/66 out of the 50--89 training window). The internal
per-band radial panel splits the working-age validation range into
50--62 / 63--75 / 76--89 thirds, with radial length proportional to excess MAE.

\section{Disclosure ledger}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Deep-layer seed direction rate 0.56.} Claims are carried at the
ensemble level (10/15, $p=0.035$); the deep member alone is noisier than the
conventional 0.80 bar.
\item \textbf{EN-500 is an ensemble member.} The 12/15, $p=0.013$ comparison is a
member-versus-frame comparison; all comparisons against non-member baselines sit
at $p\le 0.006$.
\item \textbf{External composition history.} The linear core's membership was
identified with visibility into external few-shot results in an earlier round of
the project. The neighboring composition \{EN-40, SVR-500\} tracks the core
within 0.2 years everywhere, and the paper reports all combinations. A
project-memo claim of bit-identical agreement between the core's $k$-shot table
and the earlier-round table was found to be inaccurate (e.g., ext1 $k=10$:
18.66 vs.\ 18.44 years); the frozen JSON tables are the source of record.
\item \textbf{Support coverage.} 32/64 (ext1) and 27/66 (ext2) samples lie
outside the 50--89 training support; external claims are restricted to relative
orderings under identical inputs.
\item \textbf{Attribution axis failure.} The FiLM per-gene attribution axis fails
its pre-registered split-half gate ($\rho=0.435 < 0.6$) and is reported as
failed; FiLM's claims rest on its ablation cost and module-level dimorphism
spectrum (split-half 0.70).
\item \textbf{PFKP grade correction.} PFKP's mortality association belongs to the
source cohort study's supplementary screening table (HR 2.11, $p=0.035$), not to
its main signature; the paper carries the supplementary-screen grade. The locked
replication here is directional (HR 1.20, $p=0.17$).
\item \textbf{Geometry config-dependence.} The class-centroid velocity statistic
is reported for the monotonicity-trained representation (mean 0.93, 21/25 seeds
below 1); earlier non-regularized configurations showed expansion, so the
statistic is tied to the shipped configuration.
\item \textbf{Unnominated genes.} ZNF518B and three further claims are graded
unsupported by the literature audit and stand as falsifiable model-generated
hypotheses.
\item \textbf{HVG pool overlap.} The 8{,}000-gene variance pool is an
unsupervised ranking (no age labels enter it) computed on the working-age
training samples of fold~0, which are reused by all fifteen locked splits. The
pool's membership therefore shares sample provenance with every evaluation
block. The ranking sensitivity to an alternative sample draw was not
separately quantified; the pool was frozen at project start and held constant,
so any such sensitivity acts on every arm identically. All arms, every
baseline, and the external evaluations operate on the identical pool, and the
40- and 500-gene elastic-net feature sets are nested subsets of it.
\item \textbf{Adoption-$p$ semantics.} The reported two-sided $p=0.035$ is the
monotonicity comparison on the eq3 layer (10 of 15 splits favor the adopted
configuration); the deep-member layer shares the same $p$-value at 11 of 15
splits by coincidence. On the ten design-free splits (the last ten locked
splits; the first four were design-exposed by the arm screen and one further
split was conservatively excluded), the mean lead is $+0.0299$ years with
two-sided $p=0.0059$, so the adopted configuration's advantage survives the
stricter exposure. All paper $p$-values are two-sided; one-sided
project-journal variants do not appear in the paper.
\item \textbf{GSE123696 selection.} The second external cohort (Israeli
PrimeView whole-blood microarray, $n=66$, ages 23--96) was chosen at project
start for population--platform independence from both the training cohort and
the GTEx pilot. Its earlier-round evaluation predates the final lock, so a
directional soft influence of that history on the final menu cannot be
entirely excluded; only the post-lock evaluation is reported in the paper.
\item \textbf{Survival-null specification.} The survival probe (GSE65218,
$n=151$, 70 events) is sized by availability: power for the composite
signature score reaches $\approx$ hazard ratios of 1.5 or larger only. Null
readings are therefore \emph{not-detectable at the available sample}, not
evidence of absence.
\item \textbf{No unused external queue.} The project holds no formally
registered unused third external dataset. The only candidate (GTEx muscle)
was consumed in the first round, is same-source and same-country as the GTEx
pilot, and does not qualify as an independent set. External claims are
restricted to the two independent external cohorts; should a reviewer request a
third queue, GSE65218 (used only for the survival probe) is disclosed here
together with its usage history as a semi-independent supplement.
\item \textbf{GTEx pilot history.} The GTEx blood pilot (ext1, $n=64$) entered
the project as the first external evaluation and was re-evaluated on each
pipeline round. The paper reports only the post-lock evaluation; no
historical-round external numbers are repeated.
\item \textbf{$p$-value provenance.} Split-level $p$-values come from the
repeated-split design: the fifteen 80/20 splits reuse one sample pool, so a
paired two-sided Wilcoxon test on the split-level MAEs indicates replicate
agreement under the locked design rather than independent-sample inference.
External $p$-values are paired bootstraps on external samples. No
paper $p$-value is computed on data outside the evaluations.
\item \textbf{External $p$ unadjusted.} Cell-level significance tests in the
external table are not multiplicity-adjusted across cells; they are reported
per comparison, on the conservative per-ensemble maximum basis (the maximum
over the four per-baseline paired bootstrap comparisons; ext2:
$p\le 0.033$).
\item \textbf{Collapse control provenance.} The 10.714-year collapse control is
computed under the identical fifteen locked splits and 80/20 protocol, but in
the pipeline generation preceding the final result tables, where the
elastic-net baseline was handed the extreme-age labels as ordinary regression
targets. The final tables do not include that control arm; the value is a
cross-generation structural control, not a fresh re-run on the shipped
configuration.
\item \textbf{Survival $p$ nominal.} In-band gene-level survival $p$-values
(GAL3ST4 $p=0.007$; PDZK1IP1 $p=0.095$; PFKP $p=0.17$; NT5E $p=0.85$) are
nominal and unadjusted across the ten in-band gene-level tests. None survives a
Bonferroni correction at $\alpha=0.05$ over those ten tests, and the paper
presents them as directional, corroborating evidence, not as a survival
signature.
\item \textbf{Ablation $p$ uncorrected.} Paired $p$-values in the ablation
table are uncorrected (descriptive family); the Holm correction is applied
only to the two registered comparison families.
\item \textbf{Per-ensemble-max $p$ basis.} The $p$-basis stated for the
external table is the maximum over the four per-baseline comparisons for each
ensemble (conservative); cell-level $p$-values are not reported in the paper.
\item \textbf{Holm variant.} The Holm step-down correction is computed within
each registered comparison family (the classical-baseline family; the
member-versus-frame family), not jointly across all pairwise comparisons. The
family-corrected $p$-values are the ones reported in the internal table.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Survival probe details (Vitality 90+, GSE65218)}
Cohort: 151 Finnish nonagenarians, Illumina HumanHT-12 v4, 70 deaths over the
follow-up window (Jylh\"av\"a et al., BMC Medical Genomics 7:54, 2014).
Pre-registered hypotheses, fully locked consensus weights: (H1) signature-score
Cox model adjusted for frailty and sex --- HR 1.14 (95\% CI 0.89--1.45,
$p=0.31$), confirmed by 2{,}000-label permutation; (H2) tertile Kaplan--Meier
separation --- log-rank $p=0.47$; (H3) per-gene directional agreement --- 7/10
testable genes agree with the model (70\%). Nested cross-validated recalibration
of the weights reaches C-index 0.540 against 0.585 for covariates alone
($n=151$; 70 events power only HR~$\ge$~1.5). Gene-level associations inside the
90+ band: GAL3ST4 HR 1.39 (1.09--1.76, $p=0.007$); PDZK1IP1 HR 1.21 ($p=0.095$);
PFKP HR 1.20 ($p=0.17$, directional replication of the supplementary screen);
NT5E HR 1.03 ($p=0.85$, null). Reading: the longevity signature answers band
membership, not survival within the band; the two questions decouple.
